# Memex: The Pioneer of Personal Knowledge Graphs

The Memex (a portmanteau of "memory" and "index") is the name of the hypothetical proto-hypertext system described by Vannevar Bush in his landmark 1945 article "As We May Think" in The Atlantic Monthly. 

Bush envisioned the Memex as a device in which an individual stores all their books, records, and communications, and which is mechanized so that it may be consulted with exceeding speed and flexibility. It is an enlarged intimate supplement to his memory.

The essential feature of the Memex is the concept of "associative trails" (what we now call hyperlinks). Users can join two items together, building custom trails of thought through a vast body of reference materials. This is in contrast to the rigid, hierarchical alphabetical index systems of traditional libraries.

Vannevar Bush was an American engineer, inventor and science administrator, who during WWII headed the U.S. Office of Scientific Research and Development (OSRD). His vision of the Memex directly influenced Doug Engelbart, Ted Nelson, and Tim Berners-Lee in the creation of the modern World Wide Web and hypertext systems.
